# Fortune in a Pot — candidate comparison

Judge brief (most important first): a fortune for a houseplant; funny in a way
a plant lover would laugh at (a joke that lands, not a groaned pun); kind and
warm, no doom or mockery; sounds like a fortune (prediction cadence, not a
slogan or care instruction); one line, ≤ 120 characters, reads cleanly aloud;
specific to this plant in this window beats generic.

## Candidates

1. [113] Soon a stranger will admire you from the sidewalk, and your keeper will pretend they planned your glow all along.
2. [108] This week the sun will find your favourite leaf first, and your keeper will take full credit for your shine.
3. [111] A small miracle is coming: the watering can arrives before you even ask, and your leaves will applaud in green.
4. [115] You will outgrow this pot, this window, and every doubt - but never the hand that turns you toward the morning sun.
5. [120] The gardener has been talking about you to friends again. Expect compliments, extra sunlight, and a slightly bigger pot.
6. [115] Your brightest days are ahead: the window light is yours, the drafts are done, and someone already brags about you.
7. [103] Soon a guest will point at you and say that plant is thriving - and your keeper will beam like the sun.
8. [110] Good news: the spider in the corner has decided your leaves are art, not lunch, and will guard you all winter.

## Verdict

**Chosen: #1 (113 chars).** Strongest on the brief: a real prediction
("Soon ... will ..."), the joke lands at the very end (the keeper pretending
they planned the glow all along), it is warm to both plant and keeper, and it
is unmistakably about this plant on this windowsill (a stranger admiring it
from the sidewalk). Runner-up: #2, same keeper-takes-credit joke but a
slightly smaller payoff. #8 is the funniest image but reads more like a
bulletin than a fortune; #4 is warm but barely funny; #5 sits exactly at the
120-character limit and its payoff ("slightly bigger pot") is weaker.
